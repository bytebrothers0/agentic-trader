# Advanced MSNR Video 11: Strong FTFL Entries and Obstacles

This video explains how to distinguish between a regular FTFL entry and a "Strong" FTFL entry, as well as how to manage trades around market "Obstacles."

## 1. Strong FTFL Entries (The "Next Day" Rule)
While we can wait for the market to return to an FTFL (First Time Frame Level) over several days or weeks, the **most powerful** and reliable entries occur when the market returns to the FTFL on the **very next candle**.

* **Daily Timeframe:** If a valid FTFL (like an OCL) is formed on the Daily chart, you want the market to come down and tap that FTFL on the *very next day*. 
* **Weekly Timeframe:** If a valid FTFL is formed on the Weekly chart, you want the market to retrace and tap it on the *very next week*.

If the market takes several days/weeks to return to the level, the level is considered **less powerful**. It may still work, but you should **reduce your risk** (e.g., risk 20% of your normal amount).

## 2. Obstacles
An "obstacle" is any key structural point (a swing high, swing low, Apex, or RBS/SBR) that sits between your entry point and your Take Profit (TP) target. 
* Obstacles are levels that can resist the price, preventing it from reaching your final target or reversing the trade entirely.

### Managing Obstacles
* **Timeframe Alignment:** You **only** look for obstacles on the time frame of your setup. 
  * If your trade is triggered from a Weekly key level, you only look for Weekly obstacles. You do *not* look for obstacles on the Daily or 4H charts. 
  * If your trade is triggered from a Daily key level, you look for Daily obstacles.
* **Taking Profit at Obstacles:** When the price reaches a valid obstacle on the correct timeframe, you should either:
  1. Take partial profits (e.g., close 80% of the position).
  2. Move your Stop Loss (SL) to breakeven.
  3. Close the trade entirely if the obstacle is extremely strong.
* **TP Placement:** For maximum safety, you can simply set your final Take Profit exactly at the first major obstacle on your HTF (Higher Timeframe).
