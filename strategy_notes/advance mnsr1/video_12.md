# Advanced MSNR Video 12: D1 W4 Entry Model

This video covers the final entry model: the **D1 W4 Entry Model**. 
The name is derived from the timeframes involved:
* **D1**: **D**aily key level mitigated by **1** Hour timeframe.
* **W4**: **W**eekly key level mitigated by **4** Hour timeframe.

## 1. When to Use the D1 W4 Entry Model
This entry model is highly versatile and is primarily used for:
* **Retracements / Pullbacks:** Catching trades against the main storyline when the market pulls back.
* **Confirmation Entries:** When the market did not reach your ultimate Target Time Frame Level (TTFL), you can use this model to confirm a continuation in the direction of the trend.

## 2. How the D1 Entry Model Works (Daily / 1-Hour)
To take an entry based on a Daily key level using the 1-hour chart, the following conditions must be met:

1. **Mitigation of Daily Key Level:** The 1-hour chart must mitigate a 1-Day (Daily) key level.
2. **Mitigation Rule:** This mitigation **must** happen with either a **1-hour wick** or exactly **one single 1-hour candle** breaking the level. If multiple candles break the Daily key level, the setup is invalid.
3. **1-Hour Breakout:** Following the mitigation, there must be a valid breakout on the 1-hour timeframe.

**Entry Options:**
After the 1-hour breakout, there are two ways to enter:
* **Option A (The Best & Strongest):** Enter on the 1-hour Support or Resistance equilibrium. This is considered the highest probability entry (100% strong). You should allocate the majority of your risk here.
* **Option B (The QM Entry):** Enter on the 1-hour Quasimodo (QM) equilibrium. This is considered less reliable (50% strong). Since the market uses the QM about 20-30% of the time, you can place a smaller position here (e.g., 20% of your total risk) so you don't miss the trade if the market doesn't reach the support/resistance level.

## 3. How the W4 Entry Model Works (Weekly / 4-Hour)
To take an entry based on a Weekly key level using the 4-hour chart, the process is similar but involves top-down analysis for the final entry:

1. **Mitigation of Weekly Key Level:** The 4-hour chart must mitigate a 1-Week key level.
2. **Mitigation Rule:** This mitigation must happen with either a **4-hour wick** or exactly **one single 4-hour candle**. 
3. **4-Hour Breakout:** Following the mitigation, there must be a valid breakout on the 4-hour timeframe.

**Entry Options:**
* **Option A (Strongest):** Identify the 4-hour Support or Resistance that caused the breakout. Mark this as your FTFL (First Time Frame Level) box. Then, drop down to the 1-hour timeframe to find your STFL (Second Time Frame Level) Support/Resistance inside that box. Your entry is at the 1-hour equilibrium.
* **Option B (The QM Entry):** Identify the 4-hour QM and mark it as your FTFL box. Drop down to the 1-hour timeframe to find your STFL, and take your entry at the 1-hour equilibrium.

## 4. Take Profit (TP) Targets
Where you place your Take Profit depends on the context of the trade:

* **Pullback Trades:** If you are trading a pullback (counter to the main storyline), you must exit at the **closest key level of the originating timeframe**.
  * For a **D1** trade, your TP is the closest **Daily** key level.
  * For a **W4** trade, your TP is the closest **Weekly** key level.
* **Confirmation Trades:** If you are trading in the direction of the main storyline, you can allow the trade to run to the recent swing high or swing low.

## 5. Critical Rule for Valid Breakouts
For this entry model to be valid, **the structural point (high or low) that gets broken must be formed BEFORE the market mitigates the higher timeframe key level.**

* **Valid Scenario:** The market creates a swing high, then drops to mitigate the HTF Support level, and then rallies to break that swing high.
* **Invalid Scenario:** The market mitigates the HTF Support level, *then* creates a swing high, pulls back, and breaks it. This is not a valid setup for this model, and taking it will likely result in a loss.

## 6. Missed Entries
Because this strategy is highly precise, the market will not always return to trigger your entry limit order. 
* If the market misses your entry and runs to the TP, **do nothing**. 
* Advanced MSNR ensures a high win rate by avoiding bad trades. You either get triggered into a highly probable winning trade, or you simply miss the move. Losses typically only occur when the overarching storyline is changing.
