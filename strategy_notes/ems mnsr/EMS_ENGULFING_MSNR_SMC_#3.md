# EMS Engulfing, MSNR, & SMC Strategy - Part 3

This document summarizes the core logic and visual ambiguities explained in the third video of the EMS Engulfing series. The focus is on defining valid ranges, confirming breaks of structure versus liquidity sweeps, and finding sniper entries using lower timeframes.

## 1. Top-Down Analysis & Re-entries
- **Directional Bias**: Establish the trend on a higher timeframe (e.g., Daily or 8H) based on a valid engulfing candle or a break of structure.
- **Re-entries**: Once a trend is established, you can find re-entry opportunities on lower timeframes (e.g., 8H, 4H, 30M) by looking for new engulfing candles that align with the overall trend. 
- **Target & Stop Loss**: The target is generally the next major structural high (in an uptrend). The stop loss should be placed below the most recent "strong low" for safety.

## 2. Defining Valid Ranges
A crucial part of the strategy is knowing what constitutes a valid trading range.
- **Range Boundaries**: A range is bounded by a strong low (the origin of a move that broke structure) and a strong high.
- **Invalidating a Range**: If a range is broken (e.g., the strong low is violated), that range is invalidated, and you must wait for a new valid setup to form. Do not take trades based on invalidated ranges.

## 3. Break of Structure (BOS) vs. Liquidity Sweep
Visual ambiguity often arises when determining if a level is broken or just swept.
- **Break of Structure (BOS)**: A true BOS requires a **candle body to close** above/below the previous high/low's wick. This confirms the continuation of the trend.
- **Liquidity Sweep**: If a candle only **wicks** above a high or below a low, but the body closes back inside the range, it is considered a liquidity sweep. A sweep is not a BOS; rather, it indicates the market is grabbing liquidity to move in the opposite direction. 
- **Confirmation**: A powerful setup is when the market sweeps a low (grabbing liquidity) and then immediately breaks structure to the upside (BOS). This confirms a strong shift in momentum.

## 4. Refining Entries (Sniper Entries)
To get a better risk-to-reward ratio, higher timeframe zones must be refined.
- **Identify the HTF Zone**: Mark the engulfing candle or order block on the H4, 8H, or Daily timeframe.
- **Drop to LTF**: Switch to the M30, M15, or M5 timeframe to find a smaller, unmitigated zone inside the HTF zone.
- **Execution**: When price returns to the HTF zone, do not enter blindly. Wait for it to tap the refined LTF zone. Look for a LTF confirmation, such as an internal sweep and BOS, or a fresh engulfing candle, before entering the trade.

## 5. Identifying the Correct Structural Break
In complex, choppy markets, there may be multiple minor structural breaks inside a larger range.
- **Rule of Thumb**: Focus on the **most recent** valid break of structure that occurred *after* a liquidity sweep. This ensures you are aligning with the most current momentum rather than outdated structural shifts.
- **Clear Price Action**: If the structure on your current timeframe looks messy or ambiguous, simply move up one timeframe (e.g., from 30M to 1H, or 1H to 4H). The higher timeframe usually filters out the noise and provides a cleaner view of the true structure.
