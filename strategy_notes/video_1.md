# Advanced MSNR Video 1: Foundation of Malaysian SnR

This video introduces the foundational building blocks of the Malaysian Support and Resistance (MSNR) strategy. The core principle is that all key levels are marked on the **close** of the candles.

## 1. Marking Basic Support and Resistance
The strategy relies on identifying two opposite-colored candles.

### Support
- **Visual Pattern**: A bearish candle (e.g., Red/Black) followed by a bullish candle (e.g., Green/White). This forms a "V" dip.
- **How to Mark**: The horizontal support line is drawn exactly at the **close** of the bearish candle.
- **Handling Gaps**: Sometimes the market gaps, meaning the bullish candle doesn't open exactly where the bearish candle closed. **Rule**: Ignore the gap. Always draw the support line at the **close of the bearish candle**.

### Resistance
- **Visual Pattern**: A bullish candle followed by a bearish candle. This forms an inverted "V" peak.
- **How to Mark**: The horizontal resistance line is drawn exactly at the **close** of the bullish candle.
- **Wicks**: Long wicks are important for advanced low-stop-loss/high-reward entries, but for drawing the primary key level, use the close of the body.

## 2. RBS and SBR (Role Reversal)
Role reversal happens when a support or resistance level is broken by price.

### SBR (Support Becomes Resistance)
- **Visual Pattern**: Price drops and breaks below a previously established Support line.
- **Logic**: The broken Support now turns into Resistance (SBR).
- **Expectation**: When price retraces back up to this SBR line, we expect a rejection to the downside (a Sell opportunity). No need to wait for SMC inducements or liquidity grabs; the level itself is the trigger.

### RBS (Resistance Becomes Support)
- **Visual Pattern**: Price rallies and breaks above a previously established Resistance line.
- **Logic**: The broken Resistance turns into Support (RBS).
- **Expectation**: When price retraces back down to this RBS line, we expect a rejection to the upside (a Buy opportunity).

## 3. Quasimodo (QM) / Left Shoulder
A QM is formed when an SBR or RBS level **fails** (i.e., it gets broken again).

- **Failed SBR = QM**: Support is broken -> Becomes SBR. If price retraces to the SBR but breaks above it instead of rejecting, that SBR level is now a QM level.
- **Failed RBS = QM**: Resistance is broken -> Becomes RBS. If price retraces to the RBS but breaks below it instead of rejecting, that RBS level is now a QM level.
- **Trading QM**: The QM level is also known as the "Left Shoulder." When price comes back to this QM level, it acts as a very strong Support/Resistance.

## 4. OCL (Open Close Level)
OCL is used when you have two candles of the **same color** rather than opposite colors.

- **Visual Pattern**: Two consecutive bullish candles OR two consecutive bearish candles.
- **How to Mark**: The key level (OCL line) is drawn at the **close** of the **first** candle in the sequence.
- **Application**: The OCL level acts just like a regular support or resistance level. It provides additional key levels in a strong trend where opposite-colored formations might be rare.
