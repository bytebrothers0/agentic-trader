# Advanced MSNR Video 4: Storyline and Fresh vs Unfresh Levels

This video introduces the most important concept for directional bias in the MSNR strategy: **Storyline**. It also clarifies the rules for level mitigation, referred to as **Fresh vs Unfresh**.

## 1. Storyline (Directional Bias)
In MSNR, a storyline is the established trend. You only look for buys in a Bullish Storyline and sells in a Bearish Storyline. The storyline is defined by a strict formula combining a higher timeframe (HTF) rejection and a lower timeframe (LTF) breakout.

**The Storyline Formula:**
`X Rejects X and Y Breakout`
- **X** = Higher Timeframe (Monthly, Weekly, or Daily)
- **Y** = Lower Timeframe (Weekly, Daily, or 4H respectively)

### Bullish Storyline Examples
- **Daily / 4H**: Price rejects a Daily Support level (the candle wicks into the support but closes above it). After this rejection, you drop to the 4H chart and wait for price to break out above a 4H resistance. Once the 4H breakout occurs, a Bullish Storyline is confirmed.
- **Weekly / Daily**: Price rejects a Weekly Support level. Then, on the Daily chart, price breaks above a Daily resistance.
- **Monthly / Weekly**: Price rejects a Monthly Support level. Then, on the Weekly chart, price breaks above a Weekly resistance.

### Bearish Storyline Examples
- **Daily / 4H**: Price rejects a Daily Resistance level. Then, on the 4H chart, price breaks down below a 4H support. Once this 4H breakdown occurs, a Bearish Storyline is confirmed.

> **Crucial Rule**: The initial rejection on the Higher Timeframe (X) **must** happen on a **FRESH** key level. If the level is unfresh, the storyline is invalid.

## 2. Fresh vs Unfresh Levels (Mitigation)
MSNR relies heavily on the state of a key level to determine if it is safe to trade from or to use for establishing a storyline.

- **Fresh (Unmitigated)**: A key level is considered "fresh" if it has not been touched by price since it was created. This means no subsequent candle has wicked into or tested the level.
- **Unfresh (Mitigated)**: A key level becomes "unfresh" the very first time price returns to it and touches it (even with a small wick). Once a level is touched, it is considered mitigated and is no longer reliable for future rejections.
- **Becoming Fresh Again**: If an unfresh level is eventually completely broken by price (a candle body closes through it), the level changes its role (e.g., an unfresh Support becomes an SBR). When it changes roles, it becomes a **fresh** level in its new role.

### Why This Matters
If you are looking for a Daily rejection to start a Bullish Storyline, the Daily support level being tested must be fresh. If price is bouncing off a support level that was already tested weeks ago, it is an unfresh rejection and cannot be used to confirm a storyline.
