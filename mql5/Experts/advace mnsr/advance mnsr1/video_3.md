# Advanced MSNR Video 3: Line Chart, Apex, and QM Variations

This video introduces the concept of using the Line Chart to identify Apex points, which are the most critical swing points for the MSNR strategy.

## 1. The Line Chart and Timeframes
- The instructor emphasizes switching from a Candlestick chart to a **Line Chart** to remove noise and clearly see the market structure.
- **Key Rule**: This strategy focuses heavily on higher timeframes (HTF). You should identify your Apex points on the **Monthly, Weekly, and Daily** charts. The instructor explicitly states they do not use OCL or Apex identification on the 4-hour (H4) chart or lower.

## 2. Apex (External Swing Points)
- **Definition**: An Apex is an **external** swing high or swing low on the Line Chart.
  - **Apex Support**: An external swing low.
  - **Apex Resistance**: An external swing high.
- **How to Identify**: The easiest way to identify a true Apex is to look for a swing point that led to a **breakout** of a previous structure. 
- **Internal Swings**: Small pullbacks that occur *inside* a larger leg and do not lead to a breakout are **internal swings**. These are weak and will usually fail if you try to trade them as support/resistance.

## 3. iAB (Internal Apex Break)
While internal swings usually fail, there is one exception: the `iAB` (Internal Apex Break).
- **Definition**: This is an internal swing point that actually leads to a breakout of an external Apex.
- **iAB Support**: An internal swing low that leads to an upward breakout.
- **iAB Resistance**: An internal swing high that leads to a downward breakout.
- **Trading iAB**: Because this internal point caused a structural break, it becomes a valid, strong support/resistance level.

## 4. Types of QM (Quasimodo) Based on Apex
The video details specific types of QMs that form from the breakout of Apex levels:
- **abQM (Apex Break QM)**: This forms when an Apex Resistance or Apex Support is broken. For example, if an Apex Support is broken, it becomes an SBR (Support Becomes Resistance), and if broken again, it becomes an `abQM`.
- **iabQM (Internal Apex Break QM)**: This forms when an `iAB` level is broken twice, becoming a QM.
- **ftQM (First Time QM)**: This is a crucial concept. It refers to the **first time** a key level (like an Apex Support or an RBS) becomes a QM. The very first time price approaches this newly formed QM, it is an `ftQM` and is considered highly probable.
- **stQM (Second Time QM)**: This is when a level becomes a QM for the second time. It is less reliable than the `ftQM` but can still hold price in strong trends.

## Summary
The most reliable levels in MSNR are **Apex** and **iAB** levels found on the **Daily, Weekly, and Monthly** Line Charts. Identifying these structural points allows you to find high-probability `ftQM` setups for massive Risk:Reward trades.
