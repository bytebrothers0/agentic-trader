# Advanced MSNR Video 2: Types of OCL (Open Close Level)

This video dives deep into the different types of OCLs. An OCL, as defined previously, consists of two consecutive candles of the same color, where the line is drawn on the close of the first candle.

The instructor explicitly notes that they **do not trade Engulfing OCLs** (where the second candle completely engulfs the first). They focus on four specific types of valid OCLs.

## 1. sOCL (Support OCL)
- **Definition**: An OCL that is formed immediately after a Support is created.
- **Visual**: The market forms a valid Support ("V" shape). As the price moves up from that Support, it forms two consecutive bullish candles. The close of the first bullish candle is your **sOCL**.
- **Usage**: Acts as a continuation or a strong re-entry level in an uptrend.

## 2. rOCL (Resistance OCL)
- **Definition**: An OCL that is formed immediately after a Resistance is created.
- **Visual**: The market forms a valid Resistance (inverted "V" shape). As the price drops from that Resistance, it forms two consecutive bearish candles. The close of the first bearish candle is your **rOCL**.
- **Usage**: Acts as a continuation or strong re-entry level in a downtrend.

## 3. 1 LAB OCL (1 Level Above or Below OCL)
- **Definition**: This is a standalone OCL that does not have a Support or Resistance immediately next to it.
- **Identification Rule**: 
  - Mark an OCL. 
  - Look one key level above it (if it's a bearish OCL) or one key level below it (if it's a bullish OCL).
  - If the very next key level is **another OCL**, then your marked OCL is a valid `1 LAB OCL`.
  - If the very next key level is a **Support or Resistance**, then it is **not** a valid `1 LAB OCL`.
- **Strength**: This is considered one of the strongest patterns to take a trade from.

## 4. LS OCL (Left Side OCL)
- **Definition**: An OCL that acts like a Quasimodo (QM) level.
- **Identification Rule (Must be broken twice)**:
  1. The OCL is formed.
  2. It acts as Support, but price **breaks** through it (Break 1).
  3. Price comes back up, treating it as Resistance, but **breaks** through it again (Break 2).
  4. After being broken twice, it becomes an `LS OCL`.
- **Trading the LS OCL**: The third time price approaches this level, it will act as an extremely strong level (similar to a QM). The instructor notes that this specific pattern is incredibly powerful on higher timeframes like the **Daily and Weekly**.

## Key Takeaways
- The number of trades taken per week doesn't matter; what matters is the Risk:Reward (RR) ratio. High-probability levels like `1 LAB OCL` and `LS OCL` allow for very tight stop losses and massive RR (e.g., 1:7, 1:8).
